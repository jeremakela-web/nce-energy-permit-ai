BESS FIRE SAFETY IN NORWAY — NEK 488:2024, NEK 487:2022, TEK17 AND DSB
BATTERIENERGILAGRINGSSYSTEM (BESS) — BRANNSIKKERHETSKRAV
Source 1: NEK 488:2024 (Norwegian adoption of IEC 62933-5-1, -5-2, -5-3 — safety
          requirements for grid-integrated electrical energy storage systems)
Source 2: NEK 487:2022 (Norwegian standard for electrical installations for battery
          systems, general electrical safety framework preceding NEK 488)
Source 3: TEK17 kap. 11 (Byggteknisk forskrift, chapter 11 — brannsikkerhet /
          fire safety in buildings and structures)
Source 4: DSB (Direktoratet for samfunnssikkerhet og beredskap) guidance, 2021
Authority: DSB, DiBK (Direktoratet for byggkvalitet), NEK (Norsk Elektroteknisk Komite),
           NVE (Norges vassdrags- og energidirektorat), local brannvesen
Language: Norwegian (summary in English for RAG indexing)

=== PART 1: HONEST REGULATORY BASELINE — NO PRESCRIPTIVE BESS FIRE SAFETY RULES ===

The single most important fact for permitting purposes: Norway has NO dedicated,
prescriptive fire safety regulation specifically for battery energy storage systems.
This is explicitly confirmed by SINTEF's SafeBESS research programme (2021-2025), which
reviewed the Norwegian regulatory landscape and found that BESS fire safety is handled
entirely through the general, performance-based building safety framework (TEK17) rather
than through BESS-specific prescriptive rules (of the kind found in, for example, Sweden's
BFS 2024:7 or the U.S. NFPA 855). This should be stated plainly in any Norwegian BESS
permitting report: "Norway has no BESS-specific fire safety regulation; compliance is
demonstrated through TEK17's general, performance-based safety requirements combined with
the NEK 487/488 electrical safety framework, verified project-by-project with DiBK and
local brannvesen."

Practical implication: because there is no fixed checklist of binding BESS fire safety
thresholds (no mandated setback distances, no mandated suppression technology, no
mandated ventilation rate specific to BESS), Norwegian permitting is inherently more
consultative and case-specific than in neighbouring countries. Developers must build a
documented safety case (rather than tick against a prescriptive standard) and agree it
directly with DiBK (building authority) and the local brannvesen (fire service).

=== PART 2: TEK17 KAP. 11 — PERFORMANCE-BASED APPROACH ===

TEK17 (Byggteknisk forskrift, the technical building regulation under plan- og
bygningsloven) chapter 11 sets out Norway's general, function-based fire safety
requirements for all buildings and structures, including those housing battery storage.
Rather than prescribing specific BESS parameters, TEK17 kap. 11 requires that any
installation:

- Limits the risk of fire arising and spreading, both within the installation and to
  neighbouring structures (§11-1 to §11-4, general fire safety functional requirements).
- Ensures structural fire resistance appropriate to the fire load and risk class of the
  installation (§11-8, §11-9 — materials and structural fire resistance).
- Provides for safe evacuation and rescue/firefighting access (§11-12 to §11-14).

For BESS, this means the developer (typically through a fire safety engineer, "brannteknisk
rådgiver") must produce a project-specific fire safety strategy (brannkonsept) that
demonstrates, by calculation or reference to recognised industry practice (including NEK
487/488, SINTEF SafeBESS findings, and international guidance such as IEC 62933-5-1/-2/-3),
that the TEK17 functional requirements are met. This brannkonsept is what DiBK and the
local brannvesen review and approve — there is no separate "BESS permit" as such; BESS
fire safety is folded into the ordinary byggesak (building case) process.

=== PART 3: NEK 487:2022 AND NEK 488:2024 — ELECTRICAL SAFETY FRAMEWORK ===

NEK 487:2022 ("Elektriske installasjoner for batterisystemer") is the foundational
Norwegian standard covering electrical installation safety for battery systems generally
— cabling, protection, earthing, and general installation practice for stationary battery
installations, predating and underpinning the more specific BESS-focused NEK 488.

NEK 488:2024 is Norway's national adoption of the IEC 62933-5 series:
- IEC 62933-5-1 — general safety requirements for grid-integrated electrical energy
  storage systems (EES systems).
- IEC 62933-5-2 — safety requirements specifically for electrochemical-based EES systems
  (i.e. batteries, the relevant part for Li-ion BESS).
- IEC 62933-5-3 — safety requirements for other EES technology types.

Together, NEK 487 and NEK 488 form the electrical/technical safety backbone that a
Norwegian BESS project's brannkonsept and overall safety documentation must demonstrate
compliance with, even though — as noted in Part 1 — there is no separate prescriptive
fire code layered on top of them.

=== PART 4: FEF/FEL REGULATORY CHAIN ===

Electrical installation safety for BESS sits within Norway's standard electrical
regulation chain:
- FEL (Forskrift om elektriske lavspenningsanlegg) — low-voltage electrical installations
  regulation, applicable to the battery system's low-voltage DC and auxiliary circuits.
- FEF (Forskrift om elektriske forsyningsanlegg) — electrical supply installations
  regulation, applicable to the medium/high-voltage grid-connection side of a BESS
  installation.
Both are enforced by DSB and referenced alongside NEK 487/488 in the overall electrical
safety case for the installation; a BESS project spanning both low-voltage (battery/DC
side) and higher-voltage (grid connection) equipment must satisfy both FEL and FEF as
applicable to each part of the installation.

=== PART 5: KRAFTBEREDSKAPSFORSKRIFTEN — CLASS 1 INSTALLATIONS ===

Where a BESS installation is classified under Kraftberedskapsforskriften (the Power Supply
Preparedness Regulation, administered by NVE/DSB) as a Class 1 (klasse 1) critical
installation — typically larger, grid-significant storage facilities — additional
structural fire resistance requirements apply to the buildings/enclosures housing the
critical equipment:
- EI 30 fire resistance rating (30 minutes integrity and insulation) as a minimum for
  standard Class 1 rooms/enclosures.
- EI 60 fire resistance rating (60 minutes) for higher-criticality Class 1 rooms, as
  determined by NVE/DSB classification of the specific installation's importance to
  security of supply.
Developers should confirm the Kraftberedskapsforskriften classification of their
installation early, since it directly determines which EI-rating applies to the
enclosure design and interacts with the general TEK17 brannkonsept.

=== PART 6: NVE SØKNADSVEILEDER REQUIREMENTS ===

NVE's søknadsveileder (application guide) for battery storage installations (batterianlegg)
requires that a licensing/concession application include documentation addressing:
- Overall safety concept for the installation, including fire safety, referencing the
  applicable TEK17 brannkonsept and NEK 487/488 compliance.
- Grid connection and technical integration details.
- Confirmation of Kraftberedskapsforskriften classification where relevant, and the
  corresponding fire-resistance measures under Part 5.
DSB's 2021 guidance reinforces that these elements should be coordinated jointly between
the developer's brannteknisk rådgiver, the local brannvesen, DiBK (for the byggesak) and
NVE (for the energy licensing track) rather than treated as separate silos.

=== PART 7: GAP NOTE — NFPA 855 NOT REFERENCED ===

Unlike some other jurisdictions, Norwegian regulatory guidance (TEK17, NEK 487/488, DSB
2021) does NOT reference NFPA 855 ("Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy
Storage Systems"). Developers accustomed to designing against NFPA 855 in other markets
should not assume automatic equivalence or acceptance of NFPA 855 compliance as a
substitute for the Norwegian TEK17 performance-based brannkonsept process; NFPA 855
compliance data may be useful supporting evidence within a brannkonsept but is not itself
recognised as satisfying Norwegian requirements. This should be flagged transparently in
permitting reports: "Norway's BESS fire safety regime is performance-based (TEK17) and
does not formally reference NFPA 855; international test/compliance data can support but
not replace the required Norwegian brannkonsept."

=== PART 8: WHAT A NORWEGIAN BESS BRANNKONSEPT TYPICALLY CONTAINS ===

Because there is no prescriptive checklist, developers should expect a Norwegian
brannkonsept for a BESS project to be built around a structured, engineered safety
argument rather than fixed numeric thresholds. In practice, a brannteknisk radgiver will
typically address:

1. Fire load and propagation analysis: an engineering assessment of thermal runaway
   propagation risk for the specific cell chemistry, module density, and rack
   configuration, referencing SINTEF SafeBESS research findings and IEC 62933-5-2 safety
   considerations as supporting technical basis.
2. Structural fire resistance: justification of the enclosure's fire resistance rating
   against the functional requirements of TEK17 par.11-8/par.11-9, informed by the
   Kraftberedskapsforskriften classification (EI 30/EI 60) where the installation is
   Class 1.
3. Detection and suppression strategy: a documented rationale for the chosen detection
   technology (gas/VOC, smoke, temperature) and suppression approach (active suppression,
   passive compartmentalisation, or external water supply for firefighter-led cooling),
   since none of these is separately mandated by name.
4. Evacuation and emergency access: routes and access points for personnel evacuation and
   for brannvesen (fire service) intervention, satisfying TEK17 par.11-12 to par.11-14.
5. Coordination record: documented consultation with DiBK, the local brannvesen, and
   (where Kraftberedskapsforskriften Class 1 applies) NVE, showing that each authority has
   reviewed and accepted the safety case before byggetillatelse (building permit) is
   issued.

=== PART 9: WHY THE PERFORMANCE-BASED MODEL RAISES PERMITTING RISK ===

The absence of prescriptive BESS rules is not simply a lighter regulatory burden — it
shifts risk onto the developer's documentation quality. Because DiBK and the local
brannvesen have no fixed checklist to apply, the thoroughness and technical credibility of
the brannkonsept directly determines approval timelines. Projects that submit a
brannkonsept referencing only generic building fire safety practice, without engaging
explicitly with NEK 487/488, SINTEF SafeBESS findings, and the specific
Kraftberedskapsforskriften classification, commonly face requests for supplementary
information and consequent delay. Developers should treat the brannkonsept as the central
technical deliverable of the fire safety process, not a formality layered on top of the
electrical design.

=== PART 10: KEY DEFINITIONS ===

- Brannkonsept: the fire safety strategy/concept document prepared for a specific
  building or installation, demonstrating how it satisfies TEK17's functional fire safety
  requirements; the central technical deliverable for Norwegian BESS fire safety
  compliance.
- Brannteknisk radgiver: a qualified fire safety engineer/consultant who prepares the
  brannkonsept.
- DiBK: Direktoratet for byggkvalitet, the Norwegian Building Authority responsible for
  building regulation (TEK17) and the byggesak (building case) process.
- Brannvesen: the local fire and rescue service, responsible for reviewing site-specific
  fire safety measures and emergency response.
- Kraftberedskapsforskriften: the Power Supply Preparedness Regulation, administered by
  NVE/DSB, which classifies critical power infrastructure (potentially including
  significant BESS installations) into preparedness classes (e.g. Class 1) with
  associated structural fire resistance requirements.
