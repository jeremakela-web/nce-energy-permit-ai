BESS FIRE SAFETY IN SWEDEN — BOVERKET BFS 2024:7 AND RISE RAPPORT 2023:117
BATTERIENERGILAGRINGSSYSTEM (BESS) — BRANDSÄKERHETSKRAV
Source 1: Boverkets föreskrifter BFS 2024:7 (ändring i BBR, avsnitt om brandskydd för
          energilager), binding from 1 July 2025
Source 2: RISE Rapport 2023:117 "Brandsäkerhet vid landbaserade battericontainrar och
          energilager" (RISE Research Institutes of Sweden), published November 2023 —
          national technical guidance, non-binding but widely referenced by räddningstjänst
          (fire and rescue services) and insurers when no binding rule applies
Authority: Boverket (National Board of Housing, Building and Planning), MSB (Myndigheten
           för samhällsskydd och beredskap), räddningstjänst (local fire authority)
Language: Swedish (summary in English for RAG indexing)

=== PART 1: REGULATORY STATUS AND TIMELINE ===

Sweden did not have a dedicated, legally binding fire safety standard for battery energy
storage systems (BESS) until Boverket published BFS 2024:7, an amendment to Boverkets
byggregler (BBR) that specifically addresses energilager (energy storage). BFS 2024:7
becomes binding on new applications for bygglov (building permit) and startbesked from
1 July 2025. Before that date, and for any aspect not covered by BFS 2024:7, the
non-binding RISE Rapport 2023:117 is the de facto reference used by fire authorities,
insurers and BESS developers to demonstrate a defensible level of fire safety.

Practical consequence for permitting: projects with bygglov applications submitted before
1 July 2025 may still be assessed under general BBR brandskydd requirements plus RISE
2023:117 as best practice; projects applying on or after that date must demonstrate
compliance with BFS 2024:7's binding rules. Developers are advised to design to the
BFS 2024:7 thresholds regardless of application date, since räddningstjänst increasingly
expects the stricter standard as the industry norm.

=== PART 2: BFS 2024:7 — BINDING RULES FOR INSTALLATIONS ABOVE 20 kWh ===

BFS 2024:7 sets binding technical requirements that apply once the nominal energy content
of a battery installation exceeds 20 kWh. Below this threshold, general BBR brandskydd
(fire protection) provisions for electrical installations apply without the specific
energilager clauses. Above 20 kWh, the following binding requirements apply:

1. Brandcell (fire compartment):
   - The battery installation must be enclosed in its own brandcell, physically and
     fire-technically separated from adjacent spaces and from other parts of the building
     or site.
   - The fire compartment must be dimensioned to contain a fire (including thermal
     runaway propagation) for a defined period without spreading to neighbouring
     compartments, in line with BBR's general brandcell classification system (EI-class
     depends on use and adjacency, typically EI 60 or higher for energilager brandceller).

2. Brandsluss (fire airlock / fire lock):
   - Access to the brandcell containing the battery installation must go through a
     brandsluss — an airlock space with self-closing fire doors on both sides — to
     prevent smoke and fire gases from spreading into adjacent areas or evacuation routes
     when personnel enter or exit.
   - The brandsluss also protects the battery compartment from external fire spread and
     supports safe firefighter access during an incident.

3. Ventilation — 0.5% rule:
   - Mechanical or natural ventilation of the battery compartment must be dimensioned so
     that the ventilation rate corresponds to at least 0.5% of the room volume per hour
     relative to the battery's stored energy content, specifically intended to dilute and
     safely exhaust flammable and toxic off-gases (hydrogen, VOCs, CO) released during
     early-stage thermal runaway before ignition.
   - Ventilation outlets must discharge to a safe location away from air intakes,
     occupied areas and escape routes.

=== PART 3: RISE RAPPORT 2023:117 — NATIONAL TECHNICAL GUIDANCE ===

RISE 2023:117 supplements BFS 2024:7 with detailed technical recommendations that are not
themselves legally binding but are treated as the applicable state-of-the-art by
räddningstjänst when reviewing brandskyddsdokumentation (fire protection documentation)
for BESS projects, particularly outdoor, containerized installations (battericontainrar):

1. Setback distances (skyddsavstånd):
   - RISE recommends a setback of 8 to 30 metres between a battery container/enclosure and
     neighbouring buildings, property boundaries, forest edges or other combustible
     installations, with the exact distance depending on:
     - Total energy content of the installation
     - Cell chemistry (LFP generally permits shorter distances than NMC)
     - Presence and type of fire suppression system
     - Whether adjacent structures are combustible or non-combustible
   - 8 metres is presented as a practical minimum for small, well-protected LFP
     installations; 30 metres is recommended where no active suppression is installed and
     /or larger NMC-based systems are involved.

2. Early detection — VOC/CO monitoring:
   - RISE recommends off-gas detection (VOC and carbon monoxide sensors) inside each
     battery container/module, as an early warning of thermal runaway that precedes
     visible flame or smoke, enabling earlier isolation and evacuation than
     temperature-only or smoke-only detection.
   - Detection should trigger automatic disconnection of the affected string/module and
     alert to the site owner and, where applicable, directly to räddningstjänst.

3. Suppression system recommendations:
   - RISE does not mandate a single suppression technology but recommends that
     utility-scale installations include either a fixed automatic suppression system
     (e.g. water mist, aerosol, or clean agent, matched to cell chemistry) or, at minimum,
     a dedicated external water supply/hydrant connection sized for a sustained
     firefighting operation, since Li-ion thermal runaway fires typically require large
     volumes of water for cooling rather than conventional flame suppression.
   - Passive strategies (compartmentalisation, thermal barriers between racks/modules) are
     recommended in combination with, not instead of, active detection.

=== PART 4: APPLICABLE STANDARDS ===

SS-EN IEC 62619:2022 — "Secondary cells and batteries containing alkaline or other
non-acid electrolytes — Safety requirements for secondary lithium cells and batteries, for
use in industrial applications" is the primary product safety standard referenced for the
battery cells/modules themselves in both BFS 2024:7 and RISE 2023:117. Compliance
documentation (test certificates) for the specific cell/module product is expected as
part of the brandskyddsdokumentation submitted with the bygglov application.

Svensk Solenergi SSE.R8 v1.2 — the Swedish solar energy industry association's technical
recommendation for BESS ("Rekommendation för brandskydd vid batterilager", v1.2) defines
a tiered risk categorisation. Category 3 in SSE.R8 applies to large-scale, utility/grid-
connected BESS installations (as distinct from Category 1 residential and Category 2
small commercial systems) and imposes the most stringent combination of setback,
detection, suppression and documentation requirements — in practice aligning closely with
the RISE 2023:117 recommendations above. Developers of grid-scale BESS in Sweden should
design to SSE.R8 Category 3 from the outset.

=== PART 5: GAP NOTE — NO UNIFIED MANDATORY STANDARD FOR OUTDOOR BESS PARKS ===

Honest limitation: as of mid-2025, Sweden still has no single, unified, legally binding
national standard specifically governing large outdoor BESS parks (multi-container,
utility-scale installations sited outside building envelopes, e.g. co-located with wind
or solar parks). BFS 2024:7 is formulated primarily around the traditional building-permit
model (brandcell, brandsluss, ventilation within a defined enclosure) and RISE 2023:117
and SSE.R8 are guidance/industry-recommendation documents, not statute. In practice this
means:

- Large outdoor BESS park developers must construct a bespoke brandskyddsdokumentation
  combining BFS 2024:7's binding compartment/ventilation logic (applied per container or
  per grouped enclosure), RISE 2023:117's setback and detection recommendations, and
  SSE.R8 Category 3, and agree the resulting design directly with the local
  räddningstjänst and Boverket-certified brandskyddskonsult (fire safety consultant),
  since no single checklist covers the full outdoor-park configuration.
- This should be flagged transparently in any permitting report for Swedish BESS projects:
  "No single mandatory national standard governs outdoor, multi-container BESS parks;
  compliance is assembled from BFS 2024:7 (binding, per-compartment), RISE 2023:117 and
  SSE.R8 Category 3 (non-binding guidance), agreed case-by-case with local
  räddningstjänst."
- Developers should engage räddningstjänst and a certified brandskyddskonsult early in
  the design phase, well before the bygglov application, to avoid late-stage redesign.

=== PART 6: DOCUMENTATION CHECKLIST FOR BYGGLOV APPLICATION ===

A brandskyddsdokumentation (fire protection documentation) package for a Swedish BESS
project submitted on or after 1 July 2025 should demonstrate, item by item:

1. Nominal energy content (kWh) of the installation and confirmation of whether the
   20 kWh BFS 2024:7 threshold is exceeded (in practice, essentially all utility-scale
   and most commercial BESS installations exceed this threshold, so the binding rules
   apply as the default case).
2. Brandcell classification and EI-rating of the enclosure, with structural drawings
   showing the fire compartment boundary.
3. Brandsluss design drawings showing the airlock, self-closing fire doors, and their
   EI-rating, consistent with the surrounding brandcell.
4. Ventilation calculation demonstrating the 0.5% rule is met, including discharge point
   location relative to air intakes, occupied areas and escape routes.
5. RISE 2023:117 setback distance justification (8-30 m), referencing cell chemistry,
   suppression system (if any), and combustibility of neighbouring structures.
6. VOC/CO detection system specification and its interlock with automatic disconnection.
7. Suppression system specification, or documented external water supply/hydrant
   capacity where suppression is not installed.
8. SS-EN IEC 62619:2022 test certificates for the specific cell/module product.
9. For utility-scale projects: confirmation of design against Svensk Solenergi SSE.R8
   v1.2 Category 3.
10. Sign-off record from the local räddningstjänst confirming the case-by-case
    outdoor-park design has been reviewed and accepted, given the absence of a single
    unified mandatory standard for that configuration (Part 5).

=== PART 7: COMPARISON — RESIDENTIAL/SMALL COMMERCIAL VS. UTILITY-SCALE ===

Svensk Solenergi SSE.R8 v1.2 distinguishes three categories, which is useful context for
understanding why utility-scale (Category 3) projects face materially higher requirements
than smaller systems:

- Category 1 (residential): small battery systems, typically indoors or in a domestic
  garage/utility space; minimal additional fire safety measures beyond standard
  electrical installation rules and product certification.
- Category 2 (small commercial): larger than residential but below utility scale;
  intermediate requirements, often satisfied by a subset of the BFS 2024:7/RISE 2023:117
  measures depending on exact capacity and location.
- Category 3 (large-scale/utility BESS): the full combination of BFS 2024:7 binding
  compartment/ventilation rules, RISE 2023:117 setback/detection/suppression
  recommendations, and SSE.R8's own Category 3-specific documentation requirements
  apply. Grid-connected, multi-container BESS parks should be designed to Category 3
  from the outset, since retrofitting Category 3 measures after a Category 2 design has
  been agreed with räddningstjänst requires costly structural rework of the
  brandcell/brandsluss.

=== PART 8: KEY DEFINITIONS ===

- Brandcell: a fire compartment — a part of a building fire-technically separated from
  the rest of the building so that a fire within it cannot spread beyond it (or into it
  from outside) within a defined time, expressed as an EI-class (e.g. EI 60).
- Brandsluss: a fire airlock — an intermediate space with self-closing fire doors at each
  end, used where direct access between a fire compartment and an adjoining space would
  otherwise compromise the fire compartment's integrity.
- Räddningstjänst: the Swedish municipal fire and rescue service, responsible for
  reviewing and approving brandskyddsdokumentation and for emergency response.
- Brandskyddskonsult: a certified fire safety consultant engaged by the developer to
  prepare the brandskyddsdokumentation and liaise with räddningstjänst and Boverket
  requirements throughout the bygglov process.
