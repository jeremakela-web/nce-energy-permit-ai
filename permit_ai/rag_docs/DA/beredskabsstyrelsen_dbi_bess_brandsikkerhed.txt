BESS FIRE SAFETY IN DENMARK — BEREDSKABSSTYRELSEN VEJLEDNING AND DBI GAP ANALYSIS
BATTERIENERGILAGRINGSANLÆG (BESS) — BRANDSIKKERHEDSKRAV
Source 1: Beredskabsstyrelsen vejledning om brandsikkerhed ved battericontainere og
          energilagringsanlæg, June 2023 — primary regulatory guidance
Source 2: DBI (Dansk Brand- og sikringsteknisk Institut) "Gap-analyse: Brandsikkerhed for
          BESS-anlæg i Danmark", February 2025 — technical gap analysis
Authority: Beredskabsstyrelsen (Danish Emergency Management Agency), local
           redningsberedskab (municipal fire and rescue service), DBI (technical/insurance
           standards body)
Language: Danish (summary in English for RAG indexing)

=== PART 1: LEGAL BASIS — BEREDSKABSLOVEN §34 STK. 2 ===

Danish BESS fire safety obligations are anchored in Beredskabsloven (the Emergency
Preparedness Act) §34, stk. 2, which empowers Beredskabsstyrelsen to set requirements for
installations presenting a special fire or explosion hazard. Beredskabsstyrelsen's June
2023 vejledning translates this into concrete capacity-based thresholds for battery
energy storage installations:

- Installations with a nominal energy content exceeding 1,000 kWh (1 MWh): trigger the
  vejledning's baseline set of mandatory fire safety measures (see Part 2).
- Installations exceeding 2,000 kWh (2 MWh): trigger an additional, stricter tier of
  requirements, including the formal risk assessment described in Part 3.

These thresholds apply per battery installation/enclosure grouping as assessed by the
local redningsberedskab, not necessarily per individual container, so multi-container
sites must be evaluated in aggregate where containers are close enough to constitute a
single fire risk unit.

=== PART 2: MANDATORY REQUIREMENTS ABOVE THE THRESHOLDS ===

1. Brandmeldingsanlæg (fire detection and alarm system):
   - Mandatory in all closed/enclosed containers housing battery modules, regardless of
     which threshold tier applies, once the 1,000 kWh baseline is exceeded.
   - Must provide automatic detection with signal transmission to a permanently staffed
     location (site operator or, for larger sites, directly to redningsberedskab via an
     automatic fire alarm transmission, ABA-forbindelse).

2. Fire suppression or water connection (one of the two required):
   - Either a fixed automatic suppression system appropriate to the cell chemistry
     (e.g. water mist, or a clean-agent/aerosol system compatible with Li-ion thermal
     runaway behaviour), OR
   - A dedicated water connection (brandhane/vandforsyning) sized and positioned to allow
     the redningsberedskab to conduct sustained external cooling/firefighting operations,
     recognising that Li-ion battery fires typically require prolonged water application
     rather than being extinguishable by conventional means.
   - The vejledning leaves the choice between these two options to the developer, subject
     to sign-off by the local redningsberedskab as part of site approval.

3. Setback distance:
   - A minimum setback of 10 metres is required between battery containers/enclosures and
     neighbouring buildings, property boundaries, or other fire-hazard installations,
     unless a documented risk assessment justifies and is approved with a different
     distance.

4. Formal risk assessment (required above 2,000 kWh, and recommended as good practice
   above 1,000 kWh):
   - A documented, site-specific risk assessment referencing DS/EN IEC 61936-1 ("Power
     installations exceeding 1 kV AC — Common rules") must be prepared and submitted to
     the local redningsberedskab.
   - The assessment must address thermal runaway propagation, gas venting/explosion risk
     within enclosures, access and egress for emergency responders, and coordination with
     the site's electrical safety design under DS/EN IEC 61936-1.

=== PART 3: DBI GAP ANALYSIS (FEBRUARY 2025) — DOCUMENTED SHORTFALLS ===

DBI's February 2025 gap analysis was commissioned to assess whether the June 2023
Beredskabsstyrelsen vejledning, built primarily around fire brigade access and
suppression, adequately covers the full range of BESS-specific fire risks recognised
internationally (e.g. in NFPA 855 and UL 9540A testing regimes). DBI's findings, which
should be read as an honest account of the current Danish regulatory gap rather than as
additional binding requirements, include:

1. No mandatory Battery Management System (BMS) requirement:
   - The vejledning does not require a certified or independently verified BMS with
     cell-level voltage/temperature monitoring and automatic isolation, even though a
     properly functioning BMS is widely regarded (including by DBI) as the primary line
     of defence against thermal runaway. Developers are left to rely on manufacturer BMS
     specification without a Danish regulatory floor.

2. No mandatory ventilation requirement:
   - Unlike some other jurisdictions (e.g. Sweden's BFS 2024:7 0.5% ventilation rule),
     Danish rules do not prescribe a minimum ventilation rate for off-gas (hydrogen, VOC,
     CO) dilution and safe exhaust inside battery enclosures. DBI flags this as a
     significant gap, particularly for enclosed containers with fixed suppression but no
     forced ventilation.

3. No UL 9540A large-scale fire test requirement:
   - Denmark does not require UL 9540A ("Test Method for Evaluating Thermal Runaway Fire
     Propagation in Battery Energy Storage Systems") testing or equivalent large-scale
     fire propagation testing as a condition of approval, in contrast to jurisdictions
     that reference NFPA 855. DBI recommends that developers voluntarily obtain UL 9540A
     test data for their specific container/module configuration to support the risk
     assessment required under Part 2.4, since this materially strengthens the
     evidentiary basis presented to redningsberedskab.

DBI's overall conclusion is that the June 2023 vejledning provides a workable baseline
for fire brigade access and initial response, but leaves prevention-side technical
requirements (BMS certification, ventilation, propagation testing) to market practice
and insurer requirements rather than regulation. This gap should be disclosed transparently
in permitting reports for Danish BESS projects.

=== PART 4: EU BATTERY REGULATION 2023/1542 ===

Independently of national fire safety rules, all battery energy storage systems placed
on the Danish/EU market must comply with Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (the EU Battery
Regulation), which introduces due-diligence, carbon footprint declaration, and
end-of-life/recycling obligations that apply in parallel with — not instead of — the
Beredskabsstyrelsen fire safety requirements described above. Compliance with 2023/1542
does not substitute for the fire safety risk assessment, brandmeldingsanlæg, or setback
requirements; both regimes must be satisfied independently for a Danish BESS project to
proceed to commissioning.

=== PART 5: PRACTICAL PERMITTING SEQUENCE ===

1. Determine aggregate energy content of the installation (kWh) to identify which
   threshold tier (1,000 kWh / 2,000 kWh) applies.
2. Engage the local redningsberedskab early to agree the suppression-vs-water-connection
   choice and confirm the setback distance.
3. Prepare the DS/EN IEC 61936-1-referenced risk assessment (mandatory above 2,000 kWh).
4. Voluntarily address the DBI-identified gaps (BMS specification, ventilation design,
   UL 9540A test data) to strengthen the application and reduce redningsberedskab
   queries during review.
5. Confirm EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 documentation (due diligence, carbon footprint,
   end-of-life plan) is in place alongside the fire safety dossier.

=== PART 6: WHICH THRESHOLD TIER APPLIES — WORKED EXAMPLES ===

To avoid ambiguity when scoping a project's obligations under Beredskabsloven §34 stk. 2:

- A single container or grouped installation with 600 kWh nominal capacity: below the
  1,000 kWh baseline threshold; general electrical safety rules and DS/EN IEC 61936-1
  design practice apply, but the specific brandmeldingsanlæg/suppression/setback package
  in Part 2 is not formally triggered, though redningsberedskab may still request
  equivalent measures depending on siting.
- A site with 1,500 kWh nominal capacity: baseline tier applies in full — mandatory
  brandmeldingsanlæg, suppression-or-water-connection choice, and 10 m setback (Part 2,
  items 1-3); formal risk assessment is recommended but not strictly mandatory at this
  tier.
- A site with 2,500 kWh nominal capacity: full tier applies — all of Part 2's
  requirements plus the mandatory DS/EN IEC 61936-1-referenced risk assessment (Part 2,
  item 4).
- Multi-container sites: redningsberedskab assesses aggregate energy content across
  containers sited close enough together to constitute a single fire risk unit (i.e. a
  fire in one container could plausibly propagate to or affect an adjacent one within the
  assessed timeframe). Developers should not assume that splitting capacity across many
  small containers avoids the higher tier if the containers are not adequately separated.

=== PART 7: WHY THE DBI GAPS MATTER IN PRACTICE ===

The three gaps DBI identified in February 2025 are not merely theoretical — each maps to
a documented BESS incident pattern internationally:

1. No mandatory BMS requirement: without independently verified cell-level monitoring,
   early-stage cell defects or imbalance can progress to thermal runaway before the
   brandmeldingsanlæg (which typically detects smoke/heat, a later-stage signal) responds.
   DBI recommends developers specify a BMS meeting at minimum the monitoring/protection
   functions described in IEC 62619 and IEC 62933-5-2, even though Denmark does not
   mandate this by name.
2. No mandatory ventilation requirement: enclosed containers with fixed suppression but
   no forced ventilation can experience gas accumulation (hydrogen, VOCs) from
   off-gassing before any fire is detected, creating an explosion risk independent of the
   fire risk the suppression system addresses. DBI recommends voluntary ventilation
   design referencing the same 0.5%-of-volume-per-hour logic used in Sweden's BFS 2024:7,
   pending any future Danish rule-making.
3. No UL 9540A requirement: without large-scale fire propagation test data for the
   specific container/module configuration, the risk assessment required under Part 2
   item 4 must rely on generic chemistry-level assumptions rather than configuration-
   specific evidence. DBI recommends developers obtain UL 9540A (or equivalent) test data
   voluntarily, particularly for novel or high-density module configurations.

=== PART 8: PERMITTING DOCUMENTATION CHECKLIST ===

1. Nominal energy content (kWh) and threshold tier determination (Part 6).
2. Brandmeldingsanlæg specification and, for larger sites, confirmation of ABA-forbindelse
   to redningsberedskab.
3. Suppression system specification, or water connection design and hydraulic capacity,
   whichever option is chosen.
4. Setback distance drawing (≥10 m, or documented justification for an alternative
   distance agreed with redningsberedskab).
5. DS/EN IEC 61936-1-referenced risk assessment (mandatory above 2,000 kWh).
6. Voluntary supporting documentation addressing the DBI gaps: BMS specification,
   ventilation design, and UL 9540A (or equivalent) test data.
7. EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 documentation (due diligence, carbon footprint,
   end-of-life plan).
8. Sign-off record from the local redningsberedskab.

=== PART 9: KEY DEFINITIONS ===

- Redningsberedskab: the Danish municipal fire and rescue service responsible for
  reviewing site-specific fire safety measures and responding to incidents.
- Brandmeldingsanlæg: an automatic fire detection and alarm system.
- ABA-forbindelse: an automatic fire alarm transmission connection from a site's
  brandmeldingsanlæg directly to the redningsberedskab control centre.
- DS/EN IEC 61936-1: the Danish-adopted standard for power installations exceeding 1 kV
  AC, covering common electrical safety rules referenced in the mandatory risk
  assessment.
