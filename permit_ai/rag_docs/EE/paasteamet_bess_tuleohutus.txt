BESS FIRE SAFETY IN ESTONIA — PÄÄSTEAMET GUIDANCE, TULEOHUTUSE SEADUS, EVS 812-7
AKUPANKADE TULEOHUTUSNÕUDED — BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEM (BESS) FIRE SAFETY
Source 1: Päästeamet (Estonian Rescue Board) guidance, December 2024: "Ehitistes oluliste
          tuleohutusnõuete tagamine akupankade paigaldamisel" (Ensuring essential fire
          safety requirements in buildings when installing battery banks)
Source 2: Tuleohutuse seadus (Fire Safety Act)
Source 3: Siseministri määrus nr 17 (2021) (Minister of the Interior Regulation No. 17,
          2021 — fire safety requirements for buildings)
Source 4: EVS 812-7:2018 (Estonian fire safety design standard, Part 7)
Authority: Päästeamet (Rescue Board), Siseministeerium (Ministry of the Interior)
Language: Estonian (summary in English for RAG indexing)

=== PART 1: SCOPE AND REGULATORY BASIS ===

Päästeamet's December 2024 guidance is the first Estonian document to specifically
address the fire safety of akupangad (battery banks / battery energy storage systems)
installed inside or attached to buildings, closing a gap that previously existed between
the general Tuleohutuse seadus (Fire Safety Act), the building-level technical
requirements of Siseministri määrus nr 17 (2021), and the fire safety design standard
EVS 812-7:2018. The guidance applies the existing legal framework's essential
requirements (olulised nõuded) specifically to battery installations, rather than
creating an entirely new statute — meaning compliance is achieved by satisfying
Tuleohutuse seadus and Siseministri määrus nr 17 as interpreted for battery banks by the
December 2024 guidance, together with EVS 812-7:2018 design requirements.

=== PART 2: THE >5 kWh THRESHOLD — TULETÕKKESEKTSIOON REQUIREMENT ===

The key capacity threshold in the Päästeamet guidance is 5 kWh of nominal battery energy
content:

- Below 5 kWh: the installation is treated as a minor electrical installation; general
  building electrical safety rules apply but no dedicated fire compartment is mandated.
- Above 5 kWh: the battery bank must be installed within its own tuletõkkesektsioon
  (fire compartment) rated EI 30 (30 minutes integrity and insulation), separating the
  battery installation from the rest of the building both structurally and in terms of
  fire/smoke spread.

This EI 30 tuletõkkesektsioon requirement is the central technical obligation of the
guidance and is the threshold that Estonian BESS/datakeskus/teollisuus projects with any
meaningful battery capacity (well above typical residential backup systems) will need to
satisfy as a matter of course.

=== PART 3: FIRE DOOR AND ROOM-USE RESTRICTIONS ===

Within the EI 30 tuletõkkesektsioon:

1. Fire door (tuletõkkeuks) required:
   - Access to the battery compartment must be through a fire-rated door matching the
     compartment's EI 30 classification, self-closing, to prevent fire/smoke spread
     through the access opening.

2. Room use strictly limited:
   - The compartment housing the battery bank may contain only the battery equipment
     itself and directly associated electrical equipment (inverters, battery management
     system hardware, protection/switching equipment necessary for the battery
     installation's operation).
   - No other storage, unrelated equipment, or unrelated activity is permitted within the
     same fire compartment — this prevents additional fuel load and unrelated ignition
     sources from being introduced into the battery compartment.

=== PART 4: VENTILATION AND SMOKE EXTRACTION ===

- Natural ventilation is acceptable where the compartment design provides adequate
  passive air exchange to prevent accumulation of off-gases under normal operating
  conditions.
- Where natural ventilation is not achievable (e.g. an internal compartment without
  external wall openings), mechanical smoke extraction (mehaaniline suitse
  eemaldamine) is required, sized to remove combustion/off-gas products and maintain
  tenable conditions for both normal off-gassing scenarios and in the event of an
  incipient thermal event, discharging to a safe external location.
- The choice between natural ventilation and mandatory mechanical extraction must be
  justified in the fire safety design documentation submitted as part of the building
  permit (ehitusluba) application, with reference to EVS 812-7:2018.

=== PART 5: THERMAL MANAGEMENT — 5-25°C OPERATING RANGE ===

The guidance specifies a target ambient temperature range of 5°C to 25°C within the
battery compartment for safe thermal management of the installation. Maintaining the
compartment within this range (via heating, cooling, or passive design as appropriate to
the site and climate) reduces the risk of accelerated degradation and thermal runaway
associated with both excessively low temperatures (charging risk for Li-ion chemistries)
and excessively high ambient temperatures (elevated baseline heat load reducing thermal
runaway margin). Active thermal management systems (HVAC) serving the compartment should
be designed, monitored, and where relevant interlocked with the battery management system
so that temperature excursions outside 5-25°C trigger an alarm and, if sustained,
protective action.

=== PART 6: APPLICABLE PRODUCT AND SYSTEM STANDARDS ===

Estonia has formally adopted the following IEC standards as Estonian national standards
(EVS-EN prefix), and compliance documentation for these is expected as part of the
tuleohutus (fire safety) design package for the ehitusluba application:

1. EVS-EN IEC 62619:2022 — "Secondary cells and batteries containing alkaline or other
   non-acid electrolytes — Safety requirements for secondary lithium cells and batteries,
   for use in industrial applications." This is the cell/module-level product safety
   standard; manufacturer test certificates against this standard should be included in
   the permitting file.

2. EVS-EN IEC 62933-5-1:2024 — "Electrical energy storage (EES) systems — Part 5-1:
   Safety considerations for grid-integrated EES systems — General specification." This
   is the system-level safety standard covering the installed BESS as a whole (as
   distinct from the individual cells), addressing hazards arising from system
   integration, grid interaction, and installation-level risk.

Both standards should be referenced explicitly in the fire safety design documentation
alongside the EI 30 tuletõkkesektsioon, fire door, ventilation/smoke extraction, and
thermal management requirements set out above.

=== PART 7: EU BATTERY REGULATION 2023/1542 ===

As an EU member state, Estonia applies Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (the EU Battery
Regulation) to battery energy storage systems placed on the market, covering due
diligence, carbon footprint declaration, and end-of-life/recycling obligations. This
regulation operates in parallel with, and independently of, the fire safety requirements
in Parts 1-6: satisfying EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 documentation does not remove
the need for the EI 30 tuletõkkesektsioon, fire door, ventilation, and thermal
management measures required by the December 2024 Päästeamet guidance, Tuleohutuse
seadus, Siseministri määrus nr 17 (2021) and EVS 812-7:2018 — both regimes must be
satisfied for an Estonian BESS installation to proceed to kasutusluba (occupancy/use
permit).

=== PART 8: PERMITTING SEQUENCE SUMMARY ===

1. Determine nominal battery energy content (kWh) to confirm whether the >5 kWh
   tuletõkkesektsioon threshold applies (in practice, virtually all utility-scale and
   most commercial BESS/datakeskus installations exceed this threshold).
2. Design the EI 30 fire compartment, fire door, and room-use restriction (battery and
   directly associated electrical equipment only) into the building design.
3. Select and document natural ventilation or mechanical smoke extraction, referencing
   EVS 812-7:2018.
4. Specify thermal management to maintain 5-25°C within the compartment, interlocked
   with the battery management system.
5. Compile EVS-EN IEC 62619:2022 (cell/module) and EVS-EN IEC 62933-5-1:2024 (system)
   compliance documentation.
6. Confirm EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 documentation (due diligence, carbon
   footprint, end-of-life plan) is prepared alongside the fire safety dossier.
7. Submit the combined fire safety design package with the ehitusluba (building permit)
   application; obtain Päästeamet sign-off before kasutusluba (occupancy/use permit).

=== PART 9: WORKED EXAMPLES — THRESHOLD APPLICATION ===

- A small residential battery backup system of 4 kWh: below the 5 kWh threshold; general
  building electrical safety rules apply, no dedicated EI 30 tuletõkkesektsioon is
  mandated, though good practice (adequate spacing from combustibles, basic ventilation)
  is still recommended.
- A commercial rooftop-solar-paired battery system of 30 kWh: above the 5 kWh threshold;
  full EI 30 tuletõkkesektsioon, fire door, room-use restriction, ventilation/smoke
  extraction and thermal management requirements of Parts 2-5 apply.
- A utility-scale, grid-connected BESS installation of several MWh: above the 5 kWh
  threshold by a wide margin; the same qualitative requirements apply per compartment,
  but the scale typically requires multiple EI 30 compartments (one per container or
  per grouped battery room), each independently satisfying the fire door, room-use,
  ventilation and thermal management requirements, with the overall site design agreed
  with Päästeamet before ehitusluba is granted.

=== PART 10: RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE FOUR SOURCE DOCUMENTS ===

The four source documents operate at different levels and should be read together, not
as alternatives:

- Tuleohutuse seadus (Fire Safety Act): the primary statute establishing the general
  legal obligation to ensure fire safety in buildings and installations; it does not
  itself specify the 5 kWh threshold or the EI 30 compartment requirement.
- Siseministri määrus nr 17 (2021): the ministerial regulation implementing the Fire
  Safety Act's essential requirements at building-technical level (compartmentation,
  fire doors, escape routes) for buildings generally, including those housing battery
  installations.
- EVS 812-7:2018: the Estonian fire safety design standard providing the detailed
  engineering methodology (fire compartment sizing, ventilation/smoke extraction design)
  referenced when demonstrating compliance with Siseministri määrus nr 17.
- Päästeamet December 2024 guidance: the document that ties the general framework above
  specifically to akupangad (battery banks), setting the 5 kWh threshold, the room-use
  restriction, and the 5-25°C thermal management target that are specific to battery
  installations and not found in the general building fire safety rules.

=== PART 11: KEY DEFINITIONS ===

- Tuletõkkesektsioon: a fire compartment — a part of a building fire-technically
  separated so that fire cannot spread beyond it (or into it) within the classified time,
  expressed as an EI-class (e.g. EI 30 = 30 minutes integrity and insulation).
- Tuletõkkeuks: a fire-rated door matching the fire compartment's EI classification,
  required to be self-closing.
- Päästeamet: the Estonian Rescue Board, the national authority responsible for fire
  safety guidance, inspection, and enforcement.
- Ehitusluba: building permit, issued by the local municipality following review of the
  building design including the fire safety documentation described above.
- Kasutusluba: occupancy/use permit, issued after Päästeamet inspection confirms the
  as-built installation satisfies the approved fire safety design.
